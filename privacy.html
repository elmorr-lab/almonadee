AL-MONADEE · المنادي

Privacy Policy — effective 19 November 2026 (Android: version 7.0 and later · iPhone and iPad: version 1.0 and later)

AL-MONADEE (المنادي) is free software published on Google Play and on the App Store by elmorr (the developer, “we”), as a contribution from EJAD Gulf Technologies Co. Ltd., Al Khobar, Saudi Arabia, with its permission. This policy explains what the app does with information. In short: there is no account, no advertising and no tracking, and neither the developer nor EJAD operates a server or receives any data from the app.

1. DATA WE COLLECT

None. We run no servers for this app and have no analytics, no advertising network, no crash reporting and no user accounts. We cannot identify you and we receive nothing from your device.

2. WHAT THE APP SENDS, AND TO WHOM

Prayer times are calculated on your device, from the city you chose. Your location is never sent anywhere for that. (Versions 5.2 and earlier downloaded the month's times from the Aladhan API, which received the coordinates of the chosen city; version 6.0 ended this.)

To do the rest of its job the app talks directly to a small number of public services from your device, only for the features named:

a) Clock check — the app asks a public server (api.quran.com) for the current time once in a while, to warn you if the device clock is wrong. Nothing about you is sent.

b) Finding your location (only when you press "Detect my location", or when you accept the suggestion on first run) — ipwho.is, with ipapi.co as a fallback. These services see your internet address and return an approximate city. The app does not send your address anywhere else, and the app never uses GPS.

c) City search (only when you type a city name) — Open-Meteo geocoding. The app sends the text you typed and receives matching places.

d) Qur'an text, translations, tafsir, recitation and word search (only when you use them) — Quran.com (Quran Foundation): api.quran.com and its audio servers. The app sends the verse or surah reference, the translation and the reciter you chose, so Quran.com sees which surah or ayah was asked for. The tafsir is fetched when you open it and is not stored. When you search the Qur'an by word, the words you type are sent to Quran.com to find the ayat. A surah you choose to keep for listening without a connection is stored on your device for one week and then removed by the app. The mushaf's page pictures are inside the app. Its pages in the King Fahd Complex's own print are drawn from page fonts that the app downloads from Quran Foundation's servers (verses.quran.foundation) — the font of a page the first time you open that page, or of all 604 pages if you ask for them — and keeps on your device until you remove them (Settings → Qur'an). Asking for a font tells the server only which page's font was asked for.

e) Hadith and athkar — the lists of sahih hadith and athkar shown in the app are inside it; nothing is fetched to show them. Only when you search the hadith and choose "Dorar" under the search field are the words you type sent to dorar.net, which returns the matching hadith. The "View source" link opens dorar.net in your browser only when you tap it.

f) Athan recordings (only when you download a voice) — the Islamic Network CDN.

g) Weather (only if you turn it on; it is off until you do) — the Norwegian Meteorological Institute's weather service (api.met.no). To show today's weather on the Home screen, the app sends the coordinates of the city you chose, rounded to about one kilometre, while the app is open and at most every 30 minutes. With them goes only the app's name, version and web address, as the service asks. You can turn the weather off again in Settings.

Each of these providers has its own privacy policy and will see your internet address as any website does. The app sends them nothing about you beyond what is described above, and it does not keep the words you searched for.

3. WHAT STAYS ON YOUR DEVICE

Your settings, the calculated prayer times, cached verses, downloaded Athan recordings, your reading position in the Qur'an and the place where you stopped, your bookmarks, marks and notes, your Athkar counts, the prayers and fasts you mark in "My record", surahs you keep for listening (for one week), mushaf pages you have opened or downloaded, and any photos you choose as backgrounds are stored on the device only. Home-screen widgets read the same local data. Uninstalling the app removes them.

Recordings you make with the app — your own Athan ("My Athan") and your recitation ("My recitation") — are made and kept on your device. The app never uploads them; they leave the device only if you share one yourself. The same goes for the verse and hadith cards you make: a card is sent only to the app you choose when you tap Share.

Backup. If Android's own backup is switched on for your phone, Android includes this app's small stores — your settings, your marks and notes, "My record" and your Athkar counts — in the backup of your phone that it keeps in your own Google account, and puts them back on a new phone. We never see that backup. You can also save the same things to a file yourself (Settings → Backup → Save a backup file): the file goes where you choose and nowhere else. Recordings are in neither; when you move to a new phone with Android's phone-to-phone transfer, your recitation recordings travel directly between the two phones.

4. PERMISSIONS AND WHY

• Internet and network state — to fetch verses, recitations and recordings, to search the Qur'an and hadith when you ask, and to show whether the device is online.
• Alarms and reminders (exact alarms) — to call the Athan at the exact minute the prayer time enters. This is the core purpose of the app.
• Display over other apps — to show the prayer banner over whatever is on screen at prayer time. Optional; the app works without it.
• Microphone (phones) — only when you tap Record in "My Athan" or "My recitation", to record your own voice. Android asks you the first time. The recording stays on your device. Optional; devices without a microphone are not affected.
• Media playback in the background — so a recitation you start keeps playing when you leave the app or turn the screen off. A notification shows what is playing, with pause and stop.
• Notifications — the countdown before each prayer, the notice while the Athan plays, the message when the phone is on silent, the quiet dua after the Athan, the silent lines around each prayer (the time left to the Iqamah, the sunnah prayers, "Have you prayed?", Duha and Witr — each can be switched off), the short tone at the Iqamah, the notice while the phone is quiet for a prayer, the notice that the app can free storage, the recitation that is playing, and the wake-up before Fajr if you set it.
• Full-screen notifications (phones) — to show the Athan screen, and the wake-up before Fajr if you set it, over the lock screen, like an alarm clock. Optional.
• Do Not Disturb access (phones) — only if you switch on "Silence the phone during prayer" and choose Silent rather than Vibrate. The app uses it for one thing: to put the ringer on silent at the Iqamah and back as it was afterwards. Optional; without it the phone goes to Vibrate.
• Ignore battery optimisations (phones) — only if you allow it, so the system does not delay the Athan. Optional.
• Run at startup — on a phone, so the Athan and your reminders are set again after the phone restarts (Android forgets them when it turns off); on a TV only if you turn on "Keep working after the TV restarts", so the Athan still sounds after a power cut.
• Photos — the app uses the system photo picker. It can read only the photos you pick, never your library.

5. ON IPHONE AND IPAD

The iPhone and iPad app does the same things with the same services as described above, with these differences:
• It has no “Detect my location”: you choose your city from the list or search for it by name.
• Location (only if you allow it) — on the Qibla screen you may let the app use the device's location so that the compass counts from true north. It is used on the device for that alone; it is not stored and it is never sent anywhere. Without it the compass counts from magnetic north and says so.
• Notifications — the alert at each prayer time, the reminder before it, the tone at the Iqamah, the silent lines around each prayer and the notice that the app can free storage. They are planned on the device.
• Alarms — only if you set the wake-up before Fajr. iOS asks you the first time.
• Microphone — only when you tap Record in “My Athan” or “My recitation”. iOS asks you the first time. The recording stays on your device.
• Audio in the background — so a recitation you start keeps playing when you leave the app or lock the screen.
• Widgets — they read the city and the settings that the app shares with them on the device, and send nothing.
• Backup — if you back your device up with iCloud or to a computer, Apple includes this app's data — your settings, marks and notes, “My record”, your Athkar counts and your recordings — in that backup, in your own Apple account. We never see it. Downloaded mushaf pages are left out. You can also save a backup file yourself (Settings → Backup): the file goes where you choose and nowhere else, and it can be restored in the Android app too.
• To find faults, the app keeps a short technical note of its own alerts on the device — whether iOS accepted and delivered them. It holds no place, no prayer time and no text, and it is never sent anywhere.

6. CHILDREN

The app is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

7. YOUR CHOICES

Every feature that sends anything can be switched off in Settings or is used only when you ask: the weather (off until you turn it on), the Qur'an verse and recitation, the tafsir, the voice downloads, and location detection (choose your city from the built-in list instead). The Qur'an and hadith searches send nothing until you search. Your recordings, your record, your marks and notes can be deleted in the app at any time, and downloaded mushaf pages can be removed in Settings → Qur'an; "My record" can also be switched off. Prayer times, the Qibla, the Athan, the mushaf, the Athkar and the hadith lists work entirely offline.
